GridFPV

Vision

Internal design doc · the north star

GridFPV is free, open-source, all-in-one race management for FPV drone racing — real-world racing and Velocidrone virtual racing as equals, in one self-hostable app.

This document is the stable north star: what GridFPV is, why it exists, and the principles we will not violate. It deliberately stays at the level of intent. Architecture, the timer-adapter spec, the data model, and the phase plan live in their own docs (see Architecture, Timer Adapters, and Roadmap). When this doc and an architecture decision disagree, the principles here win until we deliberately change them.

1. What GridFPV is

GridFPV is FOSS, all-in-one race management for FPV drone racing. It is not just a lap timer, and not just a bracket generator. It runs the whole event pipeline end to end: pilot setup and registration, qualifying, bracket generation, running heats live, results and standings, streaming and overlays, and pushing results back to the leagues that need them. One open application, self-hostable by any chapter, owning the entire race day.

Status at v0.4.0-alpha.1 (2026-07-05): setup/registration, qualifying (Time Trials), live heats with race audio, deep marshaling, and results/standings are shipped — as native desktop portables for Linux/Windows/macOS with the live RotorHazard adapter. Bracket builders are deliberately deferred (primitives-first carve, rebuilt post-v1 from tournaments-snapshot; hand-chained seeding covers a quali → top-N final today). Streaming/overlays, the cloud tier, and league push-back remain future (v0.6+).

Two kinds of racing are equal first-class citizens by design, not bolt-ons: real-world (IRL) racing through physical timers, and Velocidrone virtual racing. Most tools treat one as the real product and the other as an adapter glued on the side. GridFPV is built from the timing layer up so that a sim race and a physical race are the same kind of thing to the engine.

The product vision rests on five pillars:

All-in-one & FOSS. One open, self-hostable app for the entire event — no closed silo, no piecing together a timer here, a bracket tool there, and a streaming setup somewhere else.

Velocidrone + IRL as equals. A pluggable timer-adapter layer makes simulator and physical timing genuinely interchangeable. Velocidrone racing is a primary use case, not an afterthought — it is the first adapter we build, precisely because treating it as first-class forces the abstraction to be honest.

First-class MultiGP integration. Pushing event results to MultiGP RaceSync — authenticating to a chapter and sending pilots, heats, lap times, and placements — is non-negotiable for US chapter racing. It is designed in from the start, not patched on later.

A modern, user-friendly UI. The open tools that exist today are powerful but dated and clunky. A clean, approachable, modern interface is a deliberate goal and a real differentiator — race directors and pilots should enjoy using it.

Defensible results — accountability by design. Results are not disposable. Because the truth is an append-only log and the system is a deterministic fold over it, every call — a detected lap, a marshal's correction, a penalty, a lock — is a recorded, attributable, reversible fact. This is foundational, not a marshaling sub-feature; see §7.

2. The thesis

The Director app is the product and the source of truth. It runs a full race offline, owning the truth for the duration of an event. The Cloud is optional — a sink for long-term stats and a relay for racers who aren't on the local network. It is never in the critical path of a live race; if it vanishes, races still run.

The connective idea is one protocol, two transports. The Director's embedded server and the Cloud expose the same read/realtime contract, so a racer's client is identical whether it's talking to a Director on the LAN or to the Cloud from anywhere. We build the contract once and honor it on both ends.

3. Design principles

These are load-bearing. Don't violate them without an explicit decision to do so.

The Director is authoritative and offline-first. A race must run end to end with zero internet. The Director owns the truth during an event.

The Cloud is optional. It is never required to run a live race — only a stats sink and an off-LAN relay. Its absence degrades nothing that matters on race day.

One protocol, two transports. The LAN server and the Cloud expose the same read/realtime contract, so the racer client neither knows nor cares which it's talking to.

Append-only truth. Each event records raw timing as an append-only log; everything analytical is derived. New metrics can be recomputed retroactively, and a dropped sync simply resumes where it left off.

Timers are pluggable. Every timing source is an adapter behind one interface. Velocidrone is the first adapter, not a special case — that's what keeps the abstraction honest.

Open data. A pilot's data is theirs — exportable and queryable through an open API behind a free account. Open in access and portability, not a closed silo: that's the core differentiator versus closed platforms.

4. Open data as the differentiator

A pilot's racing history belongs to the pilot. GridFPV keeps raw laps and exposes them: an open read API, data export, and embeddable widgets that chapters can build their own dashboards and ranking sites on top of. Access to a Cloud — a chapter's own self-hosted instance or the project's SaaS — is behind a free account rather than anonymous; your data stays yours, portable and fully queryable, with the openness in access and portability, not anonymity. Because the truth is an append-only log and everything analytical is derived, any metric — career profiles, consistency scores, per-track personal bests, head-to-head records, cross-event skill ratings, season standings — can be computed and recomputed over the full history. The closed platforms treat your data as their moat; we treat openness as ours.

5. Integrated streaming

Broadcast is part of running a modern race, and it is a stated goal — not an accident we hope to reach. GridFPV aims for built-in streaming and overlays on par with FPVTrackside: OBS control, video capture, RTMP-style inputs, and broadcast-ready overlays (podium, leaderboards, brackets) driven directly by live race state. Most open tools do timing or streaming well, rarely both; doing both, openly, is the point.

6. Where GridFPV sits in the landscape

There are good tools in this space already. GridFPV isn't claiming they're bad — it's filling a gap none of them fill together. An honest read of the field:

Tool Strengths Where it leaves a gap
Next (go-next.co) Closed SaaS; modern UI, MultiGP integration, broad race-management features. Closed and siloed; Velocidrone is bolted on; no integrated streaming; your data lives in their platform.
LiveTime Strong, professional IRL race management; paired with LapRF gold-standard hardware. Closed/commercial; no simulator focus.
RotorHazard FOSS timing system + event front-end; MultiGP RaceSync plugin; LED/overlay integrations; excellent at IRL timing. Built around physical timing — sim isn't first-class; the UX is dated.
FPVTrackside Free, mostly open source; the streaming benchmark (OBS remote, RTMP-server input, capture/replay); supports LapRF and RotorHazard. Velocidrone only via RTMP, not native; Windows/.NET desktop; UI shows its age.

The gap, and our reason to exist: no existing tool is a FOSS, all-in-one race manager that treats Velocidrone and IRL racing as equals, with first-class MultiGP integration, a path to integrated streaming, and a modern UI. Each competitor covers a slice; none covers the whole, openly. GridFPV is built to be that whole.

Table stakes we must match

Filling the gap doesn't excuse us from the basics. To be taken seriously, GridFPV has to match what race directors already expect. Where that stands at the alpha: the guided setup wizard, seeding/advancement, frequency management, and live marshaling ship — and marshaling has become a differentiator, not a table stake: signal-as-evidence RSSI review, re-detection with tune-commit, the shared removal record, a native min-lap floor, and a full audited ruling model that no incumbent matches. The incumbents still lead on the bracket and qualifying structures (round robin, FIA-style single/double elimination — ours are deferred by decision, rebuilt post-v1), championships with automatic standings, and heat/channel push notifications. Closing those, in the open, is the work.

7. Defensible results — accountability by design

This section is no longer aspiration — it shipped in v0.4.0-alpha.1: evidence-backed RSSI review with re-detection, the provisional → official lifecycle with the Final freeze, run-scoped and effectively-once rulings, protests (window optional, OFF by default per the decided model), and the full audit trail.

Every other FPV timing system treats results as disposable — corrections overwrite data, nothing records who changed what, and "official" is a verbal convention. GridFPV is the opposite: because the event log is append-only and the whole system is a deterministic fold over it, every action — a detected lap, a marshal's correction, a penalty, a result being locked — is a recorded, attributable, reversible fact. Marshaling is evidence-backed (the raw timer signal is preserved as the ground truth you review against); results move through an explicit provisional → official lifecycle with a protest window, role-gated so only the RD commits while pilots see but cannot change the record. The payoff: results are defensible — every call traces to evidence and to a person, nothing is silently lost, disputes are settled by replaying the record. The architecture was chosen for this; marshaling is where it becomes visible.

This theme is not a new mechanism bolted on — it is what the existing foundations already buy us, named and made first-class:

Log-sourced & replay-deterministic is the enabler. The append-only spine (Architecture §3) means corrections are appended facts that reference the raw observations they adjudicate — never destructive edits. The deterministic fold means current truth is always recomputable from the record, so audit, provenance, reversibility, and recompute-after-a-ruling come natively. Every marshaling action is a logged event with who/when/source, for free.

The auth tiers are the other enabler. The protocol's control/read split (Protocol §5) makes "only the RD commits, pilots see but cannot change the record" a real, enforced boundary rather than a convention: the RD holds the privileged control path while the read-only pilot tier is first-class. Defensibility needs both — an immutable record and a role model over who may write to it.

The dedicated design is in Marshaling: timer-agnostic lap-level corrections, signal-as-evidence where the timer provides a trace, and the governance layer (provisional → official, audit trail, reversibility, roles, adjudication) that no existing system has.
